1. Working Proof of Concept ✏️
    <Aside title="Working Proof of Concept">
        I built an early version of an interactive map that tries to rethink how we measure success in 
        open source.

        Instead of just ranking projects by stars or popularity, the idea is to place them on a 2D map based 
        on two dimensions: how quickly they deliver value and how much impact they have on the broader ecosystem.
        This lets you explore projects spatially and start to see patterns in how different types of tools emerge 
        and grow.

        At this stage, the prototype shows that this idea actually works. Repositories can be meaningfully 
        spread across this space, and interesting clusters start to appear. That said, it’s still early. The 
        scoring system needs to be more grounded, and there’s a big opportunity to tell richer stories about each
         project, especially around why it was created and how it evolved.
    </Aside>

1. Functionality That Proves Feasibility ✏️
    <Aside title="Core Functionality">
        The current version focuses on proving the concept works:
            - A 2D map that places repositories using time to value score and ecosystem impact score
            - Color-coded “islands” that group projects into categories like Infrastructure, Viral Tools, and Utility
            - Hover and click interactions that surface key details like the project name, creator, and origin story
            - A simple spreading mechanism to separate overlapping points and keep the map readable
    </Aside>

1. Separation of Concerns ✏️
    <Aside title="Separation of Concerns">
        To me, separation of concerns means structuring the project so that each part has a clear responsibility 
        and can be worked on independently.

        In this prototype, I separated:
        - Data (data.json) - stores repository information, scores, and categories <br></br>
        - Rendering logic (React / App.jsx) - handles positioning and plotting of repositories <br></br>
        - UI and styling - controls the visual appearance of the map <br></br>
        - Interaction logic - manages hover and click behavior

    </Aside>

1. What I Struggled With ✏️
    <Aside title="Challenges">
        The biggest challenge was translating abstract ideas like “time to value” and “ecosystem impact” into numerical scores. 
        These concepts are subjective, so assigning values required balancing intuition with consistency.

        Another challenge was dataset distribution. Initially, most repositories clustered in the top half of the map, making the visualization 
        feel unbalanced. I had to intentionally add more repositories in the lower regions to create a more meaningful spread.
    </Aside>

1. Reflection ✏️
    <Aside title="Reflection">
        This project started as an attempt to analyze open source success through traditional metrics like stars and forks. Over time, it shifted into something more exploratory.

        The current version focuses on understanding success through a different lens. By mapping repositories across conceptual dimensions, it becomes possible to explore them 
        spatially and see patterns that aren’t obvious in raw numbers alone.

        Personally, this shift felt much more meaningful. Moving away from purely quantitative analysis toward a more story driven approach made 
        the project more engaging and, in many ways, more insightful. It surfaces not just how 
        projects perform, but what kind of impact they have and how they come to exist in the first place.
    </Aside>
