    #### Chapter 9 in Critical Web Design

    ##### Summary

    Chapter 9 explores how modern web technologies allow for the data tracking and surveillance of many internet users.The 
    chapter examines how everyday users interact with web infrastructure such as cookies, APIs, analytics tools, and 
    servers which collect and analyse of user behavior information. The book sees tracking as more than something purely 
    technical, but frames it as a socio-political issue closely tied power dynamics, wealth and techno-capitalism. It 
    discusses how surveillance can reinforce inequalities by allowing institutions to observe and influence 
    users without equivalent transparency or control. Additionally, the chapter introduces legal and ethical frameworks such 
    as GDPR and “Rights of the Data Subject,” emphasizing that users should have agency over their data. 

    ##### Quote

    In their book, Supervision, John Gilliom and Torin Monahan say we live in a sur-
    veillance society, where “virtually all significant social, institutional, or business
    activities in our society now involve the systematic monitoring, gathering, and analy-
    sis of information in order to make decisions, minimize risk, sort populations, and
    exercise power.”

    ##### Comment

    The chapter made me realize the deep power inbalance that exists as a conseequence of constant surveillance. As someone who
    has always been drawn to development, I have often justified tracking as improving personalization or analytics, but this 
    chapter challenges that assumption by reframing tracking as a design choice that redistributes power. It makes me 
    reconsider how backend logging, API requests, and analytics dashboards might unintentionally reinforce systems of 
    control rather than simply improving user experience.

    ##### Discussion Question

    Is data collection is inherently tied to power?

    ##### Related Work 

    The Netflix documentary The Social Dilemma explores these themes of data collection as they relate to power assymetry and 
    ultimately techno-capitalism. That documentary and this chapter share in common similar concerns, driving forward questions
    about the future of the web and how we can correct the great imbalance. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uaaC57tcci0

    #### Hacktivist deletes white supremacist websites

    ##### Summary

    The article tells the story of how a hacktivist named "Martha Root" who deleted 3 white supremacist websites during the annual Chaos 
    Communication Congress in Hamburg, Germany. The sites included a dating platform for extremists, a reproductive matching site for 
    white supremacists, and a labor marketplace serving these communities. During the presentation, Root demonstrated how poor 
    cybersecurity practices and weak verification systems allowed them to infiltrate the platforms using AI chatbots, scrape user data, 
    and ultimately wipe the servers hosting the sites.
