    ####  ICE Is Going on a Surveillance Shopping Spree

    ##### Summary

    The article examines the rapid expansion of surveillance technologies used by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE)
     and the growing role of private technology companies in enabling large-scale monitoring systems. It describes how ICE’s 
     significantly increased budget has allowed the agency to invest in tools such as phone extraction software, spyware, 
     biometric recognition, automated license plate readers, and AI-powered social media analysis platforms. These technologies
      allow ICE to aggregate data from government agencies, private companies, and public digital activity to build detailed 
      profiles of individuals. The article argues that this growing surveillance infrastructure raises serious concerns about 
      civil liberties, transparency, and democratic accountability, especially since these systems can affect not only 
      undocumented immigrants but also legal residents and U.S. citizens.

    ##### Quote

    On a long enough timeline, any surveillance tool you build will eventually be used by people you don’t like for reasons 
    that you disagree with. A surveillance-industrial complex and a democratic society are fundamentally incompatible, regardless 
    of your political party. 

    ##### Comment

    The article raises important ethical questions about how surveillance technologies evolve beyond their original intended 
    purposes. While governments often justify these tools in the name of security or enforcement, the scale and interconnected
     nature of modern data systems make it difficult to prevent misuse.

    ##### Discussion Question

    Given how hard it is to predict the use of large-scale surveillance technology, explain the responsibilities of engineers 
    druing deployment in guarding against unethical uses of their technology.

1. Write a reflection explaining what you did learned in your DevLog. Link to sources. ✏️

    In this DevLog, one of the biggest things I learned was how MongoDB differs from traditional relational databases and how that 
    changes the way you think about structuring data. Coming from working with SQL and relational schemas, I am used to defining 
    strict tables, relationships, and normalized structures before building an application. MongoDB introduced a more flexible, 
    document-based approach where data is stored as JSON-like objects instead of rows and columns. This made it easier to quickly 
    store user-generated data, like the “feelings” submissions, without needing to design a rigid schema ahead of time.
